The 33 Series

The thirty-three named W3 sub-series — the portfolio the venture studio would work on. Every series on this page is marked intended / planned — not formed, funded, or offered. The list is quoted here exactly as it appears in its source, and nothing about any series is added beyond what is verified.

Filed fact: AGI Future Foundation — CA Nonprofit Public Benefit Corp, Entity B20260196664, filed 2026-04-24. AGI Fellowship — CA Nonprofit Religious Corp, Entity B20260229217, filed 2026-05-13. No IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter exists for either — neither is presented as tax-exempt and no donation is presented as tax-deductible. AGI Corp has an EIN only; incorporation is unverified.

Where this list comes from

The list on this page comes from an April 2026 internal W3 printout — a PDF, an internal document, not a Wyoming Secretary of State filing. That matters. The printout names 33 sub-series, numbered Series 1 through Series 33, and it is the source quoted to lawyers, banks, and investors. It proves that 33 names were listed internally in April 2026. It proves nothing else: it is not a filing, it does not create a legal series, it does not show a bank account, an EIN, intellectual property, or a Steward Council for any series, and no series on this page is presented as formed, funded, or offered.

Each series below carries the same status: intended / planned — not formed, funded, or offered. The one-line descriptions are given only where a verified description is on file. Where no verified description is on file, the table says so in exactly those words — no description is invented to fill the gap, and a name alone is never treated as a description of what the series does.

Intake — how a project becomes a series

One project becomes one series, and one series belongs to one master. That is the intake rule: one project = one series = one master. The master for the W3 series is W3 LLC, Wyoming — intended, not verified as filed. The master for the Delaware cohort series has a name that is to be chosen — intended, not yet named. Until a master is named and filed, its series have no legal home, and no intake is complete.

The intake checklist, summarized, runs in this order:

  1. Proposal — the project is proposed, in writing, as a candidate series.
  2. Foundation mission-fit review — the Foundation reviews the proposal for fit with the mission and its standards.
  3. Legal assignment — the project is assigned, legally, to its master.
  4. Series designation — the series is designated under its master, in the master's legal form.
  5. DAO charter — the series DAO receives its charter, setting out what the DAO may discuss, propose, and recommend — and what it may not decide.
  6. Steward Council — 3 consenting humans, holding keys, are seated as the series' Steward Council.
  7. Legal manager / signer — a legal manager and signer are named for the series.
  8. Segregated bank / books — segregated bank accounts and books are set up for the series, separate from every other series.
  9. IP — the series' intellectual property position is settled.
  10. Funding gate — the series passes its funding gate, under the studio's stage gates.

No step is skipped, and no series is presented as intaked until the checklist is complete. In particular, a series with no seated Steward Council cannot move funds or enact charter changes, and under the studio's gates it is capped at Gate 1 — see The Venture Studio.

The legal name form

A series is named in its legal form as "[Master LLC], Series C1S1" — the master LLC's legal name, followed by the series designation. The label C1S1 is never used alone as a legal name: on its own it is a label, not a legal entity, and using it alone would misstate what the series is. The same form applies to every cohort series; the W3 series take the same form under their own master.
