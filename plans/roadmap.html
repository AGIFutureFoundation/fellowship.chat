Sequencing — the order of operations

The raise advice in the corporate paperwork sets a sequence, and the roadmap follows it. The sequence is: cleanup → donations → AGI Corp SAFE → first series raise. Each step depends on the one before it, and skipping a step does not make the later steps happen faster — it makes them happen without the foundation they require.

1. Cleanup. Resolve the filed-versus-intended record first: confirm filings and their status, complete missing filings (including the Fellowship Statement of Information question), put bylaws and registrations on file, settle the PBC fork by choosing one track in writing, and correct the website copy that contradicts the filings. Cleanup is not administrative tidying; it is what makes everything after it lawful to attempt.

2. Donations. The nonprofit lane comes before any securities lane: donation and grant support for the Foundation and donation and tithe support for the Fellowship, with no tax-deductibility claim until an IRS determination letter exists. A named donation route belongs here, once it exists.

3. AGI Corp SAFE. Only after cleanup and the donations lane, and only once AGI Corp's incorporation is verified and counsel has designed the offering, does the studio's SAFE become a live step — with the issuer chosen in writing and the AGI Corp SAFE and PBC SAFE treated as alternatives, never both for the same round.

4. First series raise. The first series raise — SmartCiti.X is the proposed first — comes last in this sequence, after the studio is formed and funded, the series is formed, its Steward Council is seated, its gates are defined, and counsel has reviewed the offering. A series raise before those pieces exist is not an accelerated timeline; it is a raise with no issuer, no council, and no lawful process.

Nothing in this sequence has a date attached in the files reviewed. The sequence is an order, not a calendar. When dates exist — because a filing is made, a board adopts a plan, or counsel sets a timetable — they can be added here with the filing or the minutes as the source. Until then, the roadmap shows the order and says the dates are unknown.

Stage gates — the progression framework

Every series and every studio project moves through the same progression framework: Stage 0 Idea → Stage 1 Validate → Stage 2 Build → Stage 3 Launch → Stage 4 Operate/Scale → Stage 5 Exit/Wind-down. The gates are the mechanism that keeps a project honest about where it is: a project is at the gate it has passed, not the gate it aspires to.

GateNameWhat it means
0IdeaAn idea exists on paper. Nothing is validated, built, or funded.
1ValidateThe idea has been tested against evidence: need, feasibility, and fit with the mission.
2BuildBuilding has begun under an approved plan, with humans accountable for it.
3LaunchThe work is live in its first real form, with real users or real operations.
4Operate / ScaleLive operations, sustained and growing under stewardship.
5Exit / Wind-downAn orderly exit or wind-down, with obligations settled and records kept.

Two rules govern the gates, and both are hard rules. First, a series with no Steward Council is capped at Gate 1. It can be an idea, and it can be validated — but it cannot pass into Build, because there is no council to approve the gate, no council to move funds, and no council to be accountable for the build. Since no series has a verified seated council today, no series today is past Gate 1 on this framework, whatever stage its materials may suggest.

Second, each gate needs a human approver's signature plus filed evidence. A gate is not passed by an agent's assessment, a demo, or a plan document. It is passed when a named human approver signs and the evidence for that gate is filed — the validation evidence for Gate 1, the build plan and accountability for Gate 2, the launch record for Gate 3, and so on. Without the signature and the filed evidence, the gate is not passed, and the project stays where it is. That is why the "exists today" list and the gate framework agree with each other: the worlds, fabrics, and demos that exist are real work, but the series and raises built on top of them are still at the idea and validation stages, waiting on councils, filings, and signatures that do not yet exist.

The gate framework also governs the end of a project, not just its beginning. Gate 5 — Exit / Wind-down — is a planned, orderly stage with obligations settled, not a failure state and not an afterthought. A series that reaches Gate 5 winds down under its council's authority, with the same signed-vote discipline as every other gate.

What changes this page

This page is written to be updated by evidence. A filing moves an item from "planned" to "exists" when the filing is produced. A deployment moves an item when a live check confirms it. A signed agreement moves an item when the agreement is on file. A seated council moves a series past Gate 1 when three named, consenting humans hold keys and vote. Until one of those things happens, the item stays where it is on this page — and the page stays honest about it.

The most consequential near-term changes, in the order the sequence implies, would be: resolving the Fellowship Statement of Information status; putting bylaws and AG registration on file; choosing the PBC track in writing; verifying AGI Corp's incorporation; seating the first Steward Council; standing up the public map-service endpoint that unblocks RP1; hosting the GLB model files; deploying the five Virtuals agent instances; and naming a donation route. None of those has a date in the files reviewed. Each of them is a real, achievable step — and each of them is, today, not done.
